PART 1 – Resample Filter Resampling Resampling means Selecting some
instances randomly from the dataset. Think of a class with 100 students.
Instead of checking all 100 answer papers, the teacher randomly checks
only 30 papers. That is resampling.

Why Resampling?

       Reduce dataset size

       Randomize records

       Create training datasets

       Avoid bias

Association Rule Mining After preprocessing, the next step is
Association Rule Mining Association Rule Mining: Association Rule Mining
finds relationships between items. It answers “If one thing happens,
what is likely to happen next?”

Real-life Example Suppose a supermarket observes Many customers buying

 Bread also buy

 Butter Then the rule becomes Bread → Butter This helps supermarkets
arrange products together.

More examples People buying

 Laptop also buy

 Mouse Students buying

 Notebook also buy

 Pen These are association rules.

Apriori Algorithm The experiment uses the Apriori Algorithm in WEKA.

Apriori: Apriori is the most popular algorithm for finding association
rules. It discovers

 Frequent itemsets

 Strong relationships

Example Transactions Milk Bread Milk Bread Butter Bread Butter Milk
Butter Apriori counts Milk appears 3 times Bread appears 3 times Butter
appears 3 times Then it finds Milk → Bread Bread → Butter

Milk → Butter

Important Terms 1. Support Support tells How frequently a rule occurs in
the dataset. Formula Support =Number of transactions containing A and B
——————————————– Total transactions Example 100 customers 40 purchased
Bread and Butter Support 40/100

= 40% Higher support means the rule is common.

2.  Confidence Confidence tells How reliable the rule is. Formula
    Confidence =Transactions containing A and B ——————————– Transactions
    containing A Example 60 customers purchased Bread. 40 also purchased
    Butter. Confidence 40/60 = 66.7% Meaning If someone buys Bread,
    there is a 66.7% chance they buy Butter.

Procedure in WEKA As described in the experiment: 1. Load

Apriori algorithm. 4. Run the algorithm. 5. Observe the generated rules
